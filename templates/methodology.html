Methodology

A price is only worth anything if you know where it came from and when. Here is the whole process, including the parts that fail.

Collection

Once every 24 hours an automated job requests the public pricing page of each tracked tool. It identifies itself with a named user agent and a contact URL, reads robots.txt before every host it touches, honours any Crawl-delay the site declares, and never issues more than one request every {{ generation.request_interval_sec | round | int }} seconds to the same host. If a site disallows us, we stop tracking it rather than work around it.

Extraction

From each page we pull two things:

  1. A signature — the complete set of price figures present on the page, hashed. This is deliberately crude, and that is why it is reliable: it detects that something moved even when the page has been redesigned.
  2. Per-plan figures — the amount nearest to each plan name. This is best-effort. Pricing pages are marketing pages, not APIs, and they are not built to be read by machines.

When the two disagree — the signature moved but we cannot say which plan changed — the entry is logged as Page changed and nothing more is claimed.

What we will not do

  • We do not estimate. If a figure was not on the page, the cell is blank. A plausible-looking wrong price is worse than no price.
  • We do not show stale prices as current. If we have not been able to confirm a tool's pricing for {{ generation.stale_after_days }} days, the figures are hidden and the page says so. The history stays, because history does not go stale.
  • We do not let money touch the numbers. Some tools here pay us a commission and some have no affiliate programme at all. Both are collected by the same script, on the same schedule, and shown the same way. A vendor cannot pay to be listed, delisted, ranked, or corrected.

Why prices are missing sometimes

An increasing number of vendors render pricing entirely in JavaScript, or hide it behind a region selector or a sales call. We do not execute JavaScript and we do not log in. When that is the case, the tool page will say the price could not be detected — which is itself a useful fact about that vendor.

Currency and billing period

Figures are the USD list price as shown to a visitor with no discount applied. Where a page defaults to annual billing, the annual rate is what gets recorded, and the table marks it as such. Local taxes, regional pricing, and negotiated enterprise rates are out of scope.

Corrections

If a recorded figure is wrong, that is a bug and we want to know. Report it here — include the tool and the date. Corrections are made to the collection logic, not by hand-editing the history, so the same mistake stops recurring.
